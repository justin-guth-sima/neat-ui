# Rendering bench, November 2026: the write-up

The question: is the kit's diagram renderer (with its messaging layer) clearly slower than
ngx-vflow under the same load, on the laptop and on the Modalix board? The numbers are in
`results/SUMMARY-2026-11.md` (one section per campaign), the board's frame cost in
`DIAGNOSIS-2026-11.md`, and how the measurements were checked in `VALIDATION.md`.

## Answer

**No: the kit is not clearly slower than vflow, on either machine, on either tier.**

- On the laptop the answer has teeth: the two renderers separate on ladder A, and there the kit
  holds more (smooth ×8 against none, usable at least ×32 against ×16). On B, C and D they tie.
- On the board at 1080p the answer is vacuous: neither renderer reaches either tier on ladders A
  and D, even with the lighter rungs (×1/8 to ×1/2) added for this purpose. The board's floor is
  the cost of compositing 1080p in software, not the diagram, so the rule has nothing to compare;
  the results view says "Neither meets the tier" there.
- At 1280x720 the board's floor drops and the answer has teeth on A: the kit is smooth to ×1/4
  and usable to ×1, vflow never smooth and usable to ×1/2. On D neither is smooth and both are
  usable to ×1/2, a tie. Not clearly slower, with the kit ahead on A.

The rule (spec, decided): the kit is clearly slower where its capacity is at least one doubling
step below vflow's in 2 of 3 repeats. It came out 0 of 3 on every ladder of every campaign.

## Capacities

Capacity is the largest load that held in every phase of every smaller step too (median of 3
repeats). Smooth: p95 frame within 1.25 refresh intervals, at most 5 % dropped. Usable: 30 fps or
more, p99 within 100 ms. "none" means not even the ladder's first step met the tier. Each
cell reads smooth / usable.

**Laptop** (Chromium 156, Intel GPU compositing, 1920x1080 at DPR 1; DPR native is also 1 on this
laptop, and serving the page from the board changed nothing):

| Ladder                   | kit              | vflow            | empty page       |
| ------------------------ | ---------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| A: diagram size          | ×8 / ≥ ×32       | none / ×16       | ≥ ×32 / ≥ ×32    |
| B: messages per second   | 3,200 / ≥ 25,600 | 3,200 / ≥ 25,600 | 6,400 / ≥ 25,600 |
| C: notes with rate cards | ≥ 128 / ≥ 128    | ≥ 128 / ≥ 128    | ≥ 128 / ≥ 128    |
| D: all together          | ×4 / ×8          | ×4 / ×8          | ×8 / ≥ ×16       |

×1 is the demo-sized diagram (30 blocks); ×4 on D is 120 blocks at 1,600 msg/s.

**Board** (Modalix DevKit, Cortex-A65 x16, no GPU, Chromium 151 compositing and rastering in
software, bare X at 1920x1080 60 Hz, page served on the board; rerun `mvxfbsb7`):

| Ladder                        | kit         | vflow       | empty page    |
| ----------------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| A: diagram size (×1/8 to ×32) | none / none | none / none | ≥ ×32 / ≥ ×32 |
| D: all together (×1/8 to ×16) | none / none | none / none | none / ×1     |

At the demo size (A ×1) the kit pans at 50 fps and zooms at 20, vflow at 39 and 22 (recorder;
the display presented 49/19 and 31/19). On D both renderers idle at 17 to 24 fps at every level:
any moving dot damages the whole screen. vflow freezes the page at A ×16 in every repeat (the
watchdog ends it); the kit runs to ×32. The kit's soak (ladder E, 10 phases) held 22.6 fps flat
with the heap between 7 and 13 MB.

**Board at 1280x720** (same board and build, bare X at 1280x720 60 Hz, ladders A and D, 3 repeats;
run `mvxri23v`, section 7 of the summary):

| Ladder                        | kit         | vflow       | empty page    |
| ----------------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------------- |
| A: diagram size (×1/8 to ×32) | ×1/4 / ×1   | none / ×1/2 | ≥ ×32 / ≥ ×32 |
| D: all together (×1/8 to ×16) | none / ×1/2 | none / ×1/2 | ×1/2 / ×2     |

Against 1080p, at the demo size (A ×1, recorder / presented), the kit pans at 60 / 59 where it
was 50 / 49 and zooms at 33 / 34 where it was 20 / 19; vflow pans at 40 / 39 (was 39 / 31) and
zooms at 29 / 29 (was 22 / 19). On D at ×1 the kit idles at 48 fps (was 24), vflow at 41 (was
23), and the empty page at 57 (was 45). The verdicts: A is "not clearly slower", with the kit
ahead; D is still "neither meets the tier" on smooth and a tie on usable. vflow freezes at A ×16
again, in all four loads.

## Where the board's frame time goes

From `DIAGNOSIS-2026-11.md`, in short:

- **The limit is the display compositor's single thread** (`VizCompositorThread`): software
  compositing costs about 2 ms per 256x256 content tile under the frame's damage. A full-screen
  frame costs about 41 ms (a 24 fps cap); a pan of the demo-sized diagram about 20 ms (about
  45 fps). Raster is spread over 5 to 7 threads and is not the bottleneck by itself.
- **Two kit costs sit on top and can be removed:** the zoom restyles every edge each frame (the
  `--diagram-zoom` custom property), and the dot layer's full-viewport canvas damages the whole
  screen in every frame with a dot.
- **What the kit cannot change** is the cost per damaged tile. A pan or zoom of a diagram that
  fills most of 1080p stays at about 24 to 45 fps whatever the kit draws.

## Kit proposals, ranked by measured effect

All measured on the board at A ×1 on throwaway builds; none is in the kit yet.

1. **Set `--diagram-zoom` once a gesture ends, not on every zoom event** (`zoom.ts`). Zoom 19 to
   25 fps (+30 %), the zoom's main thread 67 % to 13 % busy. About five lines. During a gesture
   the edges' hit width stays at the starting zoom (hover is not in use then).
2. **Hide the dot canvas while no dot is on its way** (`messages.ts`). Pan 45 to 50 fps once the
   traffic has stopped. Two lines.
3. **Confine the dot canvas's damage** to the drawing's on-screen bounds (or split it so damage
   follows the dots). Not measured, but by the cost model the largest lever for ladders B to D:
   it is what holds D at 17 to 24 fps. More than a small change: the canvas's mapping gets an
   offset and a resize per pan and zoom.
4. **Keep `will-change: transform` off the content**, with a comment in `diagram.css`: it costs
   11 fps of pan and 4 of zoom here, because it moves work from the raster threads to the one
   compositor thread.

Outside the kit: a 1280x720 mode for the board's display lifts the floor that the kit cannot,
and it is measured now (harness `--remote-mode 1280x720`, display restored afterwards). 720p has
44 % of 1080p's pixels and the frame cost followed about that: D idle at ×1 went from 24 to 48
fps for the kit, a frame of about 21 ms against 41 ms (a ratio of 0.5 where the pixel share is
0.44). The kit's pan at A ×1 now holds 60 fps and its zoom 33; the capacities rose from none to
smooth ×1/4 and usable ×1 on A, and to usable ×1/2 on D, for both renderers there. Not enough for
D at the demo size (kit zoom 19 fps, vflow pan 23): a moving dot still damages the whole screen,
so proposals 2 and 3 stay the lever there. Whether 720p is acceptable to show on the board is
the product's call; the bench only shows what it buys.

## Caveats

- **Board capacities at 1080p read one rung low for both renderers.** ×1/8 measures slower than
  ×1/4 (pan 43 against 52 fps, zoom 19 against 33) for the kit and vflow alike, and the empty
  page misses smooth at D ×1/8 but not at ×1/4. ×1/8 is each climb's first step, so a warm-up
  after the page load is the likely cause; not proven. Since capacity needs every smaller step
  to hold, it turns "usable ×1/4" into "none" on A. It cannot change a verdict: both renderers
  carry it. The 720p run does not show it for the kit (×1/8: pan 60, zoom 60, smooth) nor for vflow
  (usable at ×1/8, as at ×1/2), so the warm-up reading is not contradicted but not confirmed.
- **Below ×1 the diagram has no edges between frames**, so going from ×1/2 to ×1 changes the
  diagram's shape, not only its size.
- **The presented rate comes from one traced run per load** and stands for all its repeats; the
  recorder read vflow 5 to 30 % above what the display presented, so tiers use the lower of the
  two.
- **The heap is read without forcing a garbage collection**, so it shows growth, not leaks.
- **The board's page is 1919x1079** under the bare kiosk at 1080p (1279x719 at 720p; accepted),
  and the board exposes no CPU frequency, so throttling is judged by temperature only (55 to
  59 °C, none flagged).
- **Not run:** the 4K run (the board's monitor offers no 4K mode) and the loaded run with a
  pipeline on the board (it needs your go).
- **The vflow freeze at ×16** is not root-caused; a 7 s GPU-process stall in one vflow trace may
  be it.
- Thin spots in the bench itself: the `fill` round that places edges at ×1/8 has no test of its
  own, and the start-load test uses stand-in hosts.
